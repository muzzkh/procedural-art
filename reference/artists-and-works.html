Reference

Artists and Works to Study

People and pieces worth close attention, arranged by the lesson they illuminate. Use them with the study method from Special Topic 7: describe, guess the system, find the decisions, make a small study.

This list was written from memory, without checking sources, and only includes people and works I am confident exist. Dates are given only where I am sure of them. Look each one up before citing it. It is a starting point and far from complete: it leans toward well-documented work from Europe and North America, and the field is much wider than that.

By lesson

LessonArtists, works and writers
1 · Drawing with codeSol LeWitt, Bridget Riley and Vera Molnár (all above): pictures that are entirely a loop and a rule.
2 · RandomnessThe book 10 PRINT CHR$(205.5+RND(1)); : GOTO 10 (MIT Press, 2012), a whole volume on one line of code.
3 · NoiseKen Perlin, who invented the technique. Inigo Quilez's articles on noise and domain warping.
4 · Curves and trigonometryJohn Whitney and Ben Laposky (both above). Look up the harmonograph, a nineteenth-century drawing machine that makes the same curves with pendulums.
5 · Flow fieldsTyler Hobbs, Fidenza (2021), and his essays on flow fields and long-form generative art. Anders Hoff, who publishes as inconvergent.
6 · Recursion and grammarsPrzemysław Prusinkiewicz and Aristid Lindenmayer, The Algorithmic Beauty of Plants (1990). Benoit Mandelbrot, The Fractal Geometry of Nature (1982).
7 · Packing and partitioningNervous System (Jessica Rosenkrantz and Jesse Louis-Rosenberg), whose jewellery, puzzles and garments are built on cellular and growth patterns.
8 · SimulationJohn Conway's Game of Life. Alan Turing, "The Chemical Basis of Morphogenesis" (1952). Craig Reynolds's boids. Karl Sims, Evolved Virtual Creatures (1994). Sage Jenson's slime-mould simulations. Jonathan McCabe's multi-scale Turing patterns.
9 · ShadersInigo Quilez's articles and the Shadertoy site he co-founded. Patricio Gonzalez Vivo and Jen Lowe, The Book of Shaders.
10 · SystemsCasey Reas, the Process works. Dmitri Cherniak, Ringers (2021). Matt DesLauriers, Meridian (2021). Jared Tarbell, Substrate (2003). Zach Lieberman's daily sketches. The book Form+Code by Casey Reas, Chandler McWilliams and LUST (2010).
11 · ColourJosef Albers (above). Johannes Itten's colour teaching at the Bauhaus. Björn Ottosson's writing on the OKLab colour space.
12 · TypographyMuriel Cooper and the Visible Language Workshop at MIT. John Maeda, Design by Numbers (1999). The concrete poets, such as Eugen Gomringer.
13 · Images as inputKim Asendorf's pixel sorting. Chuck Close's gridded portraits. Daniel Rozin, Wooden Mirror (1999).
14 · Wave Function CollapseMaxim Gumin, who published the algorithm. Oskar Stålberg's Townscaper and Bad North. Paul Merrell's earlier work on model synthesis.
15 · AnimationJohn Whitney (above). Oskar Fischinger and Norman McLaren, for abstract animation before computers. Dave Whyte, who publishes looping animations as "bees & bombs".
16 · GrowthAndy Lomas, Cellular Forms (2014). Nervous System, Floraform. Anders Hoff's differential line studies.
17 · PhysicsThomas Jakobsen's paper "Advanced Character Physics" (2001), the source of the Verlet method used in games. Antoni Gaudí's hanging-chain models. Frei Otto's form-finding with soap films and nets.
18 · Geometry and plottingThe plotter drawings of Molnár, Mohr and Nees (above). Jürg Lehni, Hektor (2002), a spray-paint plotter.
19 · PerformanceThe demoscene, where whole animated worlds are fitted into a few kilobytes. Elevated (2009), a 4-kilobyte landscape by Inigo Quilez and collaborators, is a good place to start.

How to use this list

  • Pick one name a week. Find five works, and look at each for longer than is comfortable.
  • Write the three-line study from Special Topic 7: what you see, what you think the system is, which decisions were not the algorithm's.
  • Make one small study of one aspect, for yourself.
  • Follow their influences backward. Most of these people have said who they learned from.
  • Add your own names. A list that only contains what someone else chose is not yet yours.
