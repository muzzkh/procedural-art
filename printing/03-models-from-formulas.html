3D Printing 3

Models from Formulas

Building triangles by hand works for simple forms and becomes painful as soon as shapes must merge, hollow out or interlock. The alternative is to describe the object with one formula that says how far any point is from its surface, and let an algorithm find the triangles.

The object as a function

Lesson 9 introduced signed distance functions in two dimensions: a function that returns the distance from a point to the edge of a shape, negative inside. Everything carries over to three dimensions by adding a coordinate.

ShapeDistance function
Sphere of radius rMath.hypot(x, y, z) - r
Upright cylinderMath.hypot(x, y) - r, combined with limits on z
Slab between two heightsMath.abs(z - middle) - halfThickness
BoxMath.max(Math.abs(x) - a, Math.abs(y) - b, Math.abs(z) - c)
TorusMath.hypot(Math.hypot(x, y) - R, z) - r

The operations are the ones you know, and they are what make this method so good for printable objects:

OperationFormulaIn a workshop this would be
UnionMath.min(a, b)Gluing two parts together
Smooth unionsmin(a, b, k)Gluing with a fillet of radius about k
SubtractionMath.max(a, -b)Drilling or milling b out of a
IntersectionMath.max(a, b)Keeping only what is inside both
ShellMath.abs(a) - tHollowing to a wall 2t thick
Offseta - rGrowing the part by r, rounding its edges

Because the function knows the distance to the surface, wall thicknesses and clearances are written directly in millimetres. That is a large practical advantage over triangles.

From formula to triangles

A slicer needs triangles, so the surface where the function equals zero has to be extracted. The standard way is to sample the function on a 3D grid and look at each small cube of eight samples. If some corners are inside and some outside, the surface passes through that cube, and its position along each edge can be estimated from the two values at the ends. The method used here cuts every cube into six tetrahedra, because a tetrahedron has only a handful of cases to handle. It is called marching tetrahedra, a simpler relative of the better-known marching cubes.

MESH.fromSDF(f, min, max, cells) does this. You give it the function, the corners of the box to search, and how many grid cells to use along the longest side. About sixty lines of assets/mesh.js hold the whole algorithm, and they are written to be read.

Resolution is the trade-off. The number of samples grows with the cube of cells, so doubling it takes eight times as long. As in Lesson 2, cells much smaller than the 0.4 mm nozzle are wasted.

Try it: set the blend to its minimum to see plain unions with sharp creases. A sculpture like this has overhangs under each sphere, so it needs supports, or a larger blend so that the forms rise from the base like a candle flame.

Lattices

Special Topic 3 introduced the gyroid, a surface defined by one line of trigonometry that fills space with a strong, self-supporting lattice. With the operations above, turning it into a printable part takes three steps: take the gyroid function, give it thickness with the shell operation, and intersect it with the outer shape you want.

Lattices are used for lightweight structural parts, cushioning (printed in flexible TPU), filters, heat exchangers, and for their appearance. The cell size and wall thickness are the two engineering dials: larger cells and thinner walls are lighter and weaker.

Thickness is approximate here. The gyroid formula is not a true distance function. Dividing by 1.5 × k makes it close to one near the surface, so the wall comes out near the value you asked for, but check the result in the slicer's preview. If a wall shows as a single bead with gaps, increase it to at least 0.84 mm, which is two bead widths.

A functional object

The same toolkit makes useful things. A pen pot is a cylinder with a smaller cylinder subtracted from it above a solid floor. Texture is added by changing the outer radius with angle and height: ribs, a twist, a noise wobble. Since everything is in millimetres, the wall and floor thicknesses are simply stated, and stay correct whatever the texture does.

Notice how the constraint is handled. The wall thickness is not checked afterwards. The texture is constructed so that it cannot violate it. Designing rules into the formula, instead of testing for failures, is the most reliable way to make generators whose every output prints.

Bending space in 3D

Lesson 9's domain operations work here too. Change the coordinates before evaluating the shape:

ChangeEffect
Rotate (x, y) by an angle proportional to zTwists the object along its height
Replace x by Math.abs(x)Mirrors it: model half, get both
Wrap x with x - s * Math.round(x / s)Repeats the shape every s millimetres
Use polar angle wrapped into a wedgeRepeats it round an axis: gear teeth, petals, flutes
Add noise to the distanceRoughens the surface: stone, bark, hammered metal
Scale (x, y) by a function of zTapers or flares it

One caution: operations that stretch space unevenly make the "distance" inaccurate, which shows up as walls thinner than intended. Keep distortions gentle, and verify thickness in the slicer.

Choosing a method

Use…when…
Height mapsThe object is a relief on a flat base
LoftingIt has an axis and a changing outline: vessels, columns
Implicit surfacesShapes blend, are hollowed, or repeat as a lattice; wall thickness matters
Code-driven CAD (OpenSCAD, CadQuery)Engineering parts with exact holes, threads and flat faces
Blender geometry nodes or scriptsComplex scenes, instancing on surfaces, artistic control: see the Blender section
Direct G-codeThe effect lives in how the bead is laid, not in the shape: Lessons 4 and 5

Key ideas

  • A 3D distance function describes a solid: negative inside, zero on the surface.
  • Union, subtraction, intersection, shell and smooth blend are one line each.
  • Distances are in millimetres, so walls and clearances are stated directly.
  • Marching tetrahedra samples the function on a grid and builds triangles where it changes sign.
  • Resolution costs time by the cube. Match it to the nozzle, not to the screen.
  • Build constraints into the formula so that no output can violate them.

3D printing challenge 2

A Formula You Can Hold

Design a printable object as a single distance function, using the operations above to do something that would be hard with hand-built triangles.

Requirements

  • The object is one function f(x, y, z) in millimetres, built from at least three primitives.
  • It uses at least three different operations, including a subtraction or a shell, and a smooth blend.
  • At least one domain operation: twist, mirror, repetition or taper.
  • A stated minimum wall thickness of at least 0.84 mm that holds by construction, with a comment explaining why it cannot be violated.
  • A flat base at z = 0, and the object fits the P1S.
  • Seeded or slider-driven, with the mesh reported watertight for every setting.
  • Sliced in Bambu Studio without errors. Note whether it needs supports and why.

Ideas

  • A lattice-filled shape: a sphere, a heart, a letter, with a solid skin and a gyroid inside.
  • A desk organiser: several pots of different heights blended into one piece.
  • A phone stand, soap dish or planter with drainage holes repeated round the axis.

You are done when

  • The slicer's preview shows every wall as at least two beads.
  • Changing a slider or seed never produces an unprintable result.
