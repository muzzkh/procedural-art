3D Printing 4

G-code Fundamentals

G-code is what the printer actually runs: a plain text list of moves and settings. This lesson teaches you to read it, to calculate extrusion yourself, to add custom G-code safely through Bambu Studio, and to check any G-code before it goes near a P1S.

Read this first. A printer does exactly what G-code says, including driving the hot nozzle into the bed or into the print. Slicers protect you from that. Hand-written G-code does not.

Bambu Lab publishes no official reference for the commands specific to its printers. The P1S details in these two lessons were read from the stock P1S profile that ships with Bambu Studio (its start G-code is dated October 2025) and from community notes. Firmware and slicer updates can change them. Treat your own freshly sliced file as the authority, and watch the first layer of anything custom with a hand near the stop button.

The coordinate system of the P1S

  • Origin at the front-left corner of the plate. X runs to the right, Y toward the back, Z upward. Units are millimetres.
  • Printable area 256 × 256 mm. The stock profile allows Z up to 250 mm.
  • Excluded zone: the front-left 18 × 28 mm. The filament cutter needs it.
  • Prime line: the stock start sequence draws a purge line along the front edge, from X 18 to X 240 between Y 1 and Y 11. Keep your own first layer behind Y 15.
  • The bed moves in Z. "Z up" in G-code means the bed moving down, away from the nozzle.

The commands you need

A line is a command letter and number, followed by parameters. Anything after a semicolon is a comment.

CommandMeaning
G1 X50 Y60 E0.8 F1800Move in a straight line to X 50, Y 60 while pushing 0.8 mm of filament, at 1800 mm per minute. The core of every print.
G0 X10 Y10 F12000A travel move: the same, by convention without extruding.
G1 Z0.4Move to height 0.4 mm.
G2 / G3Clockwise and anticlockwise arcs, with the centre given as offsets I and J.
G28Home: find the mechanical origin. Must happen before any positioned move.
G90 / G91Positions are absolute coordinates, or relative steps from where the head is.
M83 / M82E values are relative (the amount for this move) or absolute (a running total). Bambu's G-code uses relative.
G92 E0Declare the current extruder position to be zero.
M104 S220 / M109 S220Set the nozzle to 220 °C, without waiting or waiting until it arrives.
M140 S60 / M190 S60The same for the bed.
M106 P1 S255Fan speed, 0 to 255. On Bambu printers P1 is the part-cooling fan, P2 the auxiliary fan, P3 the chamber fan.
M204 S5000Acceleration, in mm/s².
M220 S80 / M221 S95Scale all speeds to 80%, or all extrusion to 95%.
M400Wait until all queued moves have finished.
M400 U1Bambu: pause and wait for the user to press resume. This is the stock "pause" G-code.
M73 P25 R40Tell the screen the print is 25% done with 40 minutes left.

Two details trip up everyone. F is in millimetres per minute, so 60 mm/s is F3600. And F is sticky: once set, it applies to every following move until changed.

Extrusion: the one calculation that matters

The E value says how many millimetres of filament to push for a move. Too little and the line is thin and weak. Too much and plastic piles up and drags. The slicer computes it for every move, and if you write G-code yourself, so must you. It is a volume balance: the plastic that goes in must equal the bead that comes out.

E = (bead cross-section × move length) ÷ filament cross-section

  • Bead cross-section for a bead of width w and height h: a rectangle with rounded ends, (w − h) × h + π × (h ÷ 2)².
  • Filament cross-section for 1.75 mm filament: π × 0.875² = 2.405 mm².

For the standard 0.42 × 0.2 mm bead that works out to 0.03135 mm of filament per millimetre of travel, which is why the 60 mm sides above each have E1.881.

What is inside a Bambu Studio file

Slice any model, choose "Export G-code file", and open the result in a text editor. It has a fixed structure:

  1. Header and settings as comments: thumbnail, estimated time, filament used, and every slicer setting.
  2. The machine start G-code. On a P1S this is long, and does a great deal: heats the bed, homes, loads and purges filament (driving the AMS if present), wipes the nozzle, probes the bed, runs a vibration check, re-homes, and draws the prime line along the front edge.
  3. The layers. Each begins with the layer change G-code, which in the stock profile reports progress to the screen: M73 L… and M991 S0 P…. Then come the moves, with comments naming each feature (outer wall, inner wall, infill and so on).
  4. The machine end G-code: retract, lift, park at the back, heaters and fans off, lower the bed.

Do not write your own start and end sequences. The stock start G-code contains dozens of printer-specific steps in a deliberate order, several of them undocumented. Always reuse the one Bambu Studio writes for your printer, plate and filament. Lesson 5 shows how to combine it with a toolpath you generate.

Custom G-code through Bambu Studio

The safe way to customise a print is to let the slicer keep control and add small pieces of G-code at defined points. Bambu Studio has three places for this.

WhereRunsUse it for
Printer settings, Machine G-code: start, end, layer change, time lapse, change filament, pauseAt those events, on every print with that printer presetPer-layer logic. Save a copy of the preset first, and leave start and end alone unless you know exactly why.
Filament settings: filament start and end G-codeWhen that filament is loaded or unloadedFilament-specific settings
Preview tab: right-click the layer slider to add a pause, a filament change or custom G-codeOnce, at the layer you picked, in this print onlyOne-off actions at a height. The lowest-risk option.

In the settings fields you can use placeholders, which the slicer replaces with values when it writes the file. These appear in the stock P1S profile:

PlaceholderValue
{layer_num}The current layer, counting from 0
{layer_z}Its height in millimetres
[total_layer_count], {max_layer_z}The number of layers and the height of the top one
[nozzle_temperature_initial_layer], [bed_temperature_initial_layer_single]The filament's first-layer temperatures
{filament_type[initial_extruder]}, {curr_bed_type}The material name and the plate type
{if condition}…{endif}Include the enclosed G-code only when the condition holds

Simple techniques

Each of these is a line or two in the layer change G-code, or added at a layer in the preview.

TechniqueG-codePurpose
Pause at a layer{if layer_num == 60}M400 U1{endif}Drop in a magnet, a nut or a weight, then resume. The preview's "add pause" does the same.
Temperature tower{if layer_num == 50}M104 S215{endif}, repeated with other valuesPrint one test piece at several temperatures to find the best
Cooling by height{if layer_num == 100}M106 P1 S128{endif}Less fan for strength in one zone, more for overhangs in another
Slow a delicate sectionM220 S50 at one layer, M220 S100 at a later oneFine towers and thin features
Flow by heightM221 S95, and M221 S100 to restoreTuning; a matt-to-glossy change on some filaments
Short waitG4 P2000Dwell for 2000 milliseconds, letting a small layer cool

Whatever you change, restore it. An override left on carries into the rest of the print, and M220 and M221 in particular are easy to forget. The stock start sequence resets both, so a forgotten override will not leak into the next print, but it will affect the rest of this one.

Preflight: check before you print

A slicer checks its own output. Nobody checks yours. So write the checker once and run it on everything. A clean report means the G-code will not damage the printer in the ways tested. It does not mean the print will succeed. Always follow it with a visual check: Bambu Studio can open a G-code file and show its toolpath layer by layer.

Key ideas

  • G-code is one move or setting per line. G1 with X, Y, E and F is most of every file.
  • F is millimetres per minute, and stays in force until changed.
  • E is filament length, found from a volume balance: bead area × distance ÷ filament area.
  • Bambu printers use relative extrusion and need their own long start sequence. Reuse it, never rewrite it.
  • For simple changes, add G-code through the slicer: at a layer in the preview, or with placeholders in the layer change field.
  • Run a preflight check on any G-code you wrote or changed.

Practice

Three exercises

  • Annotate a real file. Slice a 20 mm cube, export the G-code, and find: the end of the start sequence, the first move of the first layer, a layer change, a retraction, a travel move, and the first line of the end sequence. Calculate the E value of one wall move yourself and compare it with the file.
  • Temperature tower. Using the layer change G-code and {if}, make a tall thin test piece print in five temperature bands. Work out which layer numbers begin each band from the layer height.
  • Extend the checker. Add three rules to the preflight example: extrusion before any temperature has been set, a first layer faster than 50 mm/s, and a move that passes through the prime-line strip at the front of the bed.
