Lesson 17

Physics

Lesson 5 moved particles with forces. This lesson connects them. Join points with constraints and you get ropes, cloth and jelly, all from one small method that is stable, simple, and forgiving enough to play with.

Verlet integration

Lesson 5 stored a position and a velocity for each particle. Verlet integration stores a position and the previous position instead. The velocity is never kept: it is simply the difference between the two.

velocity = position - previous
previous = position
position = position + velocity + acceleration

This looks like a minor rearrangement. Its consequence is large. Because velocity is derived from positions, you can move a point directly, to push it out of a wall or pull it toward a neighbour, and its velocity updates itself to match. Collisions and connections become a matter of correcting positions, with no forces to compute and nothing to blow up. That is why this method is used for cloth, hair and ragdolls in games.

Friction is one multiplication: scale the derived velocity by a number slightly below 1.

Notice that no line of that code makes a ball bounce or slow down when it hits something. A ball that is pushed out of the floor simply ends the step higher than it otherwise would have, so its next derived velocity points less downward.

Distance constraints: the rope

The one constraint that builds everything else is the stick: two points that must stay a fixed distance apart. After moving the points, measure the distance between each connected pair. If it is wrong, move both points along the line between them, half the error each, until it is right.

Fixing one stick disturbs its neighbours, so the fix is repeated several times per frame. More repetitions make the material stiffer. Fewer make it stretchy. A point that should not move, a pin, is simply put back in place each time.

A chain of points joined by sticks is a rope.

Cloth

A grid of points with sticks between horizontal and vertical neighbours is cloth. Nothing else is added: the same integration, the same constraint. Pin a few points along the top and it hangs. Push the points with a force that varies in space and time, such as noise, and it billows.

Real cloth simulations add diagonal sticks to resist shearing and longer ones to resist bending. To let cloth tear, delete any stick that has stretched to more than about three times its length.

Soft bodies

Close a rope into a ring and brace it, and you have a body that holds its shape and wobbles when struck. The bracing here is three sets of sticks: each point to its neighbour, to the neighbour after that, and straight across to the point opposite. Soft sticks across the middle let the body squash. Stiff ones round the rim keep it from collapsing.

From physics to pictures

Physical simulation is a design tool, not just a toy. Some ways it is used in still images and objects:

UseHow
Draped formsDrop cloth over invisible shapes, freeze it, and draw the folds as contour lines
Settled arrangementsLet objects fall and come to rest, giving packings that look placed by gravity
Spring layoutsConnect related items with springs and let the network relax into a balanced layout
Hanging chainsThe curve of a hanging chain, turned upside down, is the ideal shape for an arch. Architects have designed vaults this way.
TracesAttach a pen to a swinging or bouncing point and keep the line it draws
Motion designFollowers on springs, secondary motion, things that settle (Maths 4)

For rigid objects that tumble and stack, with friction and proper collisions between arbitrary shapes, use a physics library. Matter.js and Planck.js are the usual choices in two dimensions, and Rapier in three. Writing that well is a large job. Verlet points and sticks cover a surprising amount without one.

Key ideas

  • Verlet integration keeps position and previous position. Velocity is their difference.
  • Because of that, you can correct positions directly and the motion stays consistent.
  • A constraint is a rule about positions, enforced by moving points until it holds.
  • The stick, a fixed distance between two points, builds ropes, cloth and soft bodies.
  • Repeat the constraint pass several times. More passes mean stiffer material.
  • A pin is a point that is put back every pass.

Common mistakes

  • Setting a point's position without thinking about its previous position, which gives it a huge accidental velocity. To teleport a point, set both.
  • Too few constraint passes, so that cloth stretches like rubber, or far too many, wasting time.
  • Gravity so strong, or steps so large, that points pass through thin obstacles in a single step.

Challenge 17

Something That Hangs, Swings or Wobbles

Build a physical system from points and sticks and turn it into a piece, either an interactive toy or a still image made from the simulation's traces.

Requirements

  • Your own Verlet integrator and stick constraint, written by you.
  • At least 150 points connected into structures: a mobile of hanging shapes, a net, a curtain, a creature.
  • At least two kinds of constraint: sticks plus pins, a floor, a boundary shape, or collisions.
  • A varying force: wind from noise, the pointer, or gravity that changes direction.
  • The structure is generated from a seed, not laid out by hand.
  • It stays stable: nothing explodes or drifts away, however it is prodded.
  • Either it responds to the pointer in a satisfying way, or a key freezes it and exports a still made from the paths its points traced.

Hints

  • Write three small functions: integrate, constrain and render. Every structure then differs only in how its points and sticks are created.
  • If it jitters, add passes or reduce the forces. If it stretches, add passes.
  • For traces, store a short history of positions for a few chosen points and draw them as fading lines.

Stretch goals

  • Tearing: sticks that break when over-stretched.
  • A walker: a soft body that moves itself by changing some of its sticks' lengths in rhythm.
  • Export the frozen structure as an STL relief, using the printing lessons.

You are done when

  • You have tried to break it for a minute and failed.
  • The motion looks like a material you could name: silk, rope, rubber, jelly.
